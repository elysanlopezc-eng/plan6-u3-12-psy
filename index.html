Fictional Workplace Conflict Prompt (5 minutes)

Scenario Hook

The Marketing Disagreement

"Two senior copywriters in a branding agency disagree strongly: Ana wants to launch a bold, controversial social media campaign to spike engagement, while Carlos argues it risks alienating corporate clients and damaging long-term trust."

How should the team lead or mediator handle this professional friction before it impacts productivity?

Session Goal

Master interpersonal communication styles, analyze conflict origins, and execute structured negotiation methods.

Montano Habit

Seek First to Understand, Then Be Understood: Listening for underlying interests rather than locked-in positions.

Sources of Conflict & Active Listening (15 minutes)

Core Theory

Common Workplace Conflict Sources

  • Resource Scarcity: Competing budgets, creative tools, or tight deadlines.
  • Role Ambiguity: Unclear task ownership between departments (e.g., Sales vs. Marketing).
  • Personality & Communication Clashes: Direct vs. indirect communication styles.
  • Goal Divergence: Short-term viral metrics vs. long-term brand equity.

Active & Assertive Communication

  • Active Listening: Paraphrasing colleague statements without interrupting ("So what you're saying is...").
  • Assertive Statements: Using "I" statements ("I feel concerned when timelines shift because...") rather than blame ("You always delay").
  • Respectful Feedback: Separating the person from the creative problem.

Advanced Lexicon: Interpersonal friction, constructive divergence, framing, mediation, emotional escalation, underlying interests.

• Emphasize how poor communication turns task-oriented disagreements into destructive emotional conflicts.

Role-Play & Conflict Handling Styles (30 minutes)

Interactive Practice

The 5 Conflict Handling Styles (Thomas-Kilmann)

Avoiding: Ignoring or postponing conflict (low assertiveness, low cooperation).
Accommodating: Yielding to the other party's wishes (low assertiveness, high cooperation).
Competing: Forcing one's own solution (high assertiveness, low cooperation).
Compromising: Splitting the difference; mutual concessions (moderate both).
Collaborating: Finding a win-win integration of both needs (high assertiveness, high cooperation).

Team Role-Play Task

In pairs, role-play the marketing disagreement between Ana (creative risk-taker) and Carlos (brand safety advocate):

  • Round 1: Reenact a competing/arguing dynamic. Notice emotional buildup.
  • Round 2: Reenact a collaborating dynamic using active listening and interest-based negotiation to find a hybrid campaign model.
Montano Habit: Think Win-Win (Creating marketing solutions that satisfy both creativity and safety).
